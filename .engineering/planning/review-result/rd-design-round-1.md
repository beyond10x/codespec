Your questions, answered directly:
1. Splitting C into two stories is sound in principle: the two lists don't overlap, together they cover all 68 `untested` features, and the `depends_on` edge runs the right way. But the line is drawn in the wrong place (string handling, see the `story:reader-c-kit-plain` finding), and the "writer's fixed forms" design conflicts with the c-read crate doc rule that it infers no type of its own.
2. Go: the `fact.*` features have no form and `decl.union` has none. For `type.unit`, `expr.zero` and `intrinsic.sorted_keys`, the Go reader writes something else on purpose. `stmt.lookup_else` has a Go form only by copying the `lookup-guard` pass.
3. Java and Groovy: they do collide, both through the shared corpus (Groovy reads `java/Case.java` whenever `groovy/Case.groovy` is missing, conform.rs:87) and through `adapters/jvm`. `story:reader-java-kit` guards against it; `story:reader-java-quarkus-refusals` does not.
4. No cycles, and no feature is owned by two stories.

What I read: 8 artifacts (`aep plan artifact show`), plus `relations`, `graph` (the whole store) and `validate` (it reports the store valid). I walked 13 in-set edges (7 `decomposes`, 6 `depends_on`) and found no cycle and no chain that forces one order on the whole set. I traced 9 acceptance reads to a producer in the set, and an edge records all of them (the floor reaches `story:reader-java-kit` and `story:reader-c-kit-plain` through the chain). The Groovy report has no producer in the set.

What I could not establish: whether `adapters/jvm/src/codespec/run/Runner.java` serves both JVM runners (not opened). Outside my lane (parallel safety/scope): the Go reader change regenerating `examples/*` is in no story's list of shared files.
